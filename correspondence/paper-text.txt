 LOW-LYING ZEROS OF NEWFORMS IN THE DEPTH ASPECT AT THE
              PRIME 2, SPLIT BY ROOT NUMBER

                                          PRAGYAN MANADHATA


       Abstract. We set an even weight k ≥ 2 and study newforms at increasingly large levels N = 2n
       for both even and odd exponents n. Without assumption of any Riemann Hypothesis, we show
       that the natural one-level density of the full newspace agrees with orthogonal symmetry for
       even Schwartz tests with compact Fourier support in (−3/2, 3/2), with error Ok,ϕ (log(k2 N )−1 ).
       The proof of this result requires the use of the fact that all elliptic terms within the newspace
       trace cancel completely, except for when (N/4) | t2 − 4m. Under the Generalized Riemann
       Hypothesis for primitive Dirichlet L-functions, the root number families +1 and −1 have
       SO(even) and SO(odd) densities, respectively, for support in (−4/3, 4/3), along with the same
       error Ok,ϕ (log(k2 N )−1 ). Additionally, we determine the logarithmic mean, 1/2, of the associated
       murmuration density as well as its oscillations of order y 1/4 .

1.2. Prior work and proof strategy. For restricted Fourier support and without assumption
of any Riemann Hypothesis, Shin–Templier [ST16, Examples 9.10 and 9.14, Theorem 11.5] found
natural one-level densities in the weight aspect at level one. Under the Generalized Riemann
Hypothesis for modular, symmetric-square, and Dirichlet L-functions, Barrett et al. [BBD+ 17,
Theorem 1.3] obtain natural newform averages with support (−2, 2) at arbitrary levels. However,
their result concerns the combined family, and does not split the family by root number at
general level [BBD+ 17, Remark 1.4].
   For a fixed odd prime, Burrin et al. [BKL+ 26, Theorem 1.1] prove an unconditional mur-
muration theorem at odd conductor exponents. Tomczak [Tom26, Theorem 1.1(i)] treats even
exponents under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for all Dirichlet L-functions. Both papers
average over a fixed range of prime-to-conductor ratios. Under the Generalized Riemann Hypoth-
esis, Kroesche [Kro23, Theorem 1.2.7] obtains support beyond 1 for selected families with a fixed
root number. Here, every newform at level 2n receives equal weight, instead of using Petersson
weights like Kroesche.
   In order to remove the oldform contributions for the unsigned average, we apply a second
difference to Popa’s trace formula [Pop18, Theorem 3]. Stabilization of the local projective-root
counts forces (N/4) | t2 − 4m. Counting the pairs of primes and trace indices that satisfy
this restriction then gives support below 3/2. For the signed trace, Dirichlet GRH gives a
uniform B 3/4+ϵ error in prime averaging. This estimate works over the growing trace-index range
and gives support below 4/3. The main term of the signed one-level density comes from the
logarithmic mean of the murmuration density (Section 6). Support beyond 3/2 is known for
harmonic prime-level averages, as per Devin et al. [DFS25, Theorem 1.1].
